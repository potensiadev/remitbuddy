## 📋 Is It Legal to Get Paid in Cash in Korea?

**Short answer**: It depends on your situation.

### Legal Cash Payments

| Situation | Legal? | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Small business, under 5 employees | ✅ Yes | Common in restaurants, farms |
| Daily labor (일용직) | ✅ Yes | Construction, factories |
| Part-time work (아르바이트) | ✅ Yes | Must still be reported |
| Regular employment, avoiding taxes | ❌ No | Both employer and worker at risk |

**The key issue**: Cash payment isn't illegal. **Not reporting income** is illegal.

### What This Means for You

If your employer:
- Reports your salary to the tax office → **You're fine**
- Doesn't report (to avoid taxes) → **Both of you have a problem**

> ⚠️ **Warning**: If your employer pays cash to avoid taxes, you could lose your visa status if caught. This guide assumes your income is properly reported.

## 💰 How Much Cash Can You Deposit in Korea?

Korean banks monitor large cash deposits. Here's what triggers attention:

### Bank Reporting Thresholds

| Amount | What Happens |
|--------|--------------|
| Under ₩10,000,000 | Usually no questions |
| ₩10,000,000+ single deposit | Bank may ask for documentation |
| ₩20,000,000+ per month | Automatic report to Financial Intelligence Unit |
| Multiple deposits avoiding threshold | "Structuring" - this is illegal |

### The "Structuring" Warning

**Never do this**:
- Deposit ₩9,000,000 on Monday
- Deposit ₩9,000,000 on Wednesday
- Deposit ₩9,000,000 on Friday

This is called **"structuring"** (분할 입금) - deliberately splitting deposits to avoid reporting. Banks detect this pattern, and it's a serious offense.

**Do this instead**:
- Deposit your actual salary when you receive it
- Keep documentation of where the money came from
- Be consistent with your deposit pattern

## ⚠️ What If the Bank Asks Questions?

Don't panic. Bank staff asking questions is normal. Here's how to handle it:

### Common Questions and Answers

**Q: "Where did this money come from?"**
> A: "It's my monthly salary. I work at [company name] in [city]."

**Q: "Do you have documentation?"**
> A: Show your employment contract or recent pay stub.

**Q: "Why is it cash?"**
> A: "My employer pays in cash. It's a small company."

### Red Flags That Cause Problems

Banks get suspicious when they see:

| Red Flag | Why It's Suspicious |
|----------|---------------------|
| Large deposits with no job | Where's the money from? |
| Inconsistent amounts | Doesn't match regular salary |
| Immediate transfer after deposit | Looks like money laundering |
| Multiple accounts, same pattern | Structuring suspicion |
| Refusing to answer questions | What are you hiding? |

### If Things Get Complicated

If the bank freezes your account or asks for an investigation:

1. **Stay calm** - This happens, it's not automatic guilt
2. **Gather all documentation** - Employment contract, pay stubs, work records
3. **Contact your employer** - They may need to verify your employment
4. **Consider legal help** - Free legal aid for foreign workers: 1350 (다국어 상담)

## ❌ What NOT to Do

### Mistake 1: Using Someone Else's Account

**Don't**: Ask a friend to deposit and send money for you.

**Why**: This looks like money laundering. Both of you could get in trouble.

### Mistake 2: Using Informal Transfer Services

**Don't**: Use underground money transfer ("hawala" / 환치기).

**Why**:
- It's illegal in Korea
- No protection if money is lost
- Can result in visa cancellation

### Mistake 3: Lying About the Source

**Don't**: Tell the bank the money is a "gift" when it's your salary.

**Why**: If investigated, the lie makes everything worse.

### Mistake 4: Hoarding Cash at Home

**Don't**: Keep months of salary in cash, then deposit all at once.

**Why**: Large irregular deposits trigger investigations. Deposit regularly.

## ❓ FAQ: Cash Salary and Vietnam Transfers

### Is it illegal to receive cash salary in Korea?

No. Cash payment is legal. The issue is whether the income is reported for taxes. If your employer reports your salary properly, you're fine.

### How much cash can I deposit without questions?

Deposits under ₩10,000,000 usually don't trigger questions. But consistency matters more than amount. A regular ₩2,000,000 deposit every month is normal.

### Will remittance apps work if I'm paid in cash?

Yes. The apps don't care how you got paid. They only need:
- Money in your Korean bank account
- Valid ARC card
- Recipient details in Vietnam

### What if my employer doesn't give pay stubs?

Create your own records. Keep notes of: date, amount, work period. Text messages or KakaoTalk confirming payment also work as evidence.

### Can I send cash directly without depositing first?

No. Korean remittance apps require bank account funding. You must deposit cash first, then transfer.

### How do I explain large deposits to the bank?

Be honest: "This is my salary from my employer [name]. I work in [industry]. Here's my employment contract." Simple and truthful.

### What happens if the bank freezes my account?

Stay calm. Gather documentation. Contact your employer for verification. Call 1350 for multilingual legal support. Most freezes are resolved with proper documentation.
